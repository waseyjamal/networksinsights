Add Watermark to Image puts a text watermark on a JPG, PNG or WebP picture, once in a corner or tiled across it, and saves it in the same format. Photographers, sellers and designers use it to mark a picture with a name or a copyright line before sharing it, so a copy that travels keeps its credit. You choose the size, colour, opacity and angle, and the picture is drawn again on your device without an upload.

## How to use

1. Drop one JPG, PNG or WebP picture on the box, or click it to choose one.
2. Type the **Watermark text**, such as your name or a copyright line, up to 100 characters.
3. Set the **Text size** as a percent of the picture's shorter side, the **Opacity** from 5 to 100%, and the **Rotation** in degrees.
4. Choose a **Position**: one of the four corners, the centre, or tiled over the whole picture.
5. Pick a **Colour**, then press **Add watermark**. Check the preview, then press **Download**.

## Examples

A 4,000 by 3,000 pixel `beach.jpg` with the text `© Example Studio`, size 5%, white at 50% opacity, bottom right, gives `beach-watermarked.jpg` at the same 4,000 by 3,000 pixels. The text is 150 pixels tall, because 5% of 3,000 is 150, and it sits 75 pixels in from the bottom and right edges. Choose tiled with a rotation of -30 and the line repeats in rows across the whole picture, every copy slanted, with a gap of one text height between rows.

## Limits

Only text watermarks: a logo or image watermark cannot be uploaded in this version. One picture at a time, up to 25 MB and 50 megapixels. JPG and WebP are saved again at 92% quality, our own choice, so they lose a little; PNG is lossless. Safari cannot write WebP, so in Safari a WebP picture is saved as PNG and the result says so. The text uses your device's own sans-serif font, so it looks a little different on each system. A large rotated corner watermark can run past the edge. A watermark discourages copying but anyone with an image editor can crop or paint over it.

## FAQ

### Which opacity is hard to remove but still easy on the eye?

There is no single right value. As a rule of thumb, not a tested figure, a tiled watermark at a low opacity of 15 to 25% covers the whole picture while letting it show through; try a few and compare the previews.

### Are the camera details in my photo kept?

No. The picture is drawn again on a canvas, which writes only the pixels, so the camera model, the date and any location stored in the original are not carried over.

### Why is my phone photo upright in the result when it looked sideways elsewhere?

The browser reads the orientation tag a camera stores and draws the picture the way it should be seen, so the result is saved upright.
