Image to SVG traces a JPG, PNG or WebP picture into an SVG vector file, with 2, 4, 8 or 16 colours, inside your browser. It groups the pixels into the colours you choose, follows the edge of every patch of colour, and writes each patch as a filled path. The picture is never uploaded: the tracing runs in a background worker on your device, and the page shows the size of the SVG before you save it.

## How to use

1. Choose how many colours the SVG may use: 2, 4, 8 or 16. Each colour becomes one layer of shapes, so more colours keep more of the picture and make a larger file.
2. Drop a JPG, PNG or WebP image on the box, or click it to choose one. A picture above about 2 megapixels is first scaled down, and the page says to what size.
3. Look at the preview and at the SVG size shown with it. Pick another number of colours to trace the same picture again.
4. Press Download SVG to save the file, named after your image.

## Examples

A 40 by 40 pixel white PNG with a red square from pixel 10 to pixel 30, traced with 2 colours, gives this file of two paths:

```xml
<svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" width="40" height="40" viewBox="0 0 40 40">
<path fill="rgb(255,255,255)" d="M 0 0 L 40 0 L 40 40 L 0 40 L 0 0 Z M 10 10 L 10 30 L 30 30 L 30 10 L 10 10 Z"/>
<path fill="rgb(255,0,0)" d="M 10 10 L 30 10 L 30 30 L 10 30 L 10 10 Z"/>
</svg>
```

Notice that the white background is one path with a square hole cut in it, and the red square is a second path that fills the hole. Every colour is written as plain red, green and blue numbers.

## Limits

- It accepts JPG, PNG and WebP files up to 30 MB and 50 megapixels. Above about 2 megapixels the picture is scaled down before tracing, and the SVG has the scaled size.
- The SVG holds at most the number of colours you chose. Gradients and fine texture are cut into flat patches, and a busy photo can give a large file with many shapes.
- The SVG holds only an svg element and filled paths: no text, no embedded picture, no script and no link to another file.

## FAQ

### Is my image uploaded to a server?

No. Your browser decodes the picture, and a worker on your own device traces it with the imagetracerjs library, which this site serves itself. Nothing about the image is sent anywhere.

### Why is my image smaller in the SVG?

Pictures above about 2 megapixels are scaled down before tracing, so the work and the file stay a sensible size. The page tells you the original size and the size that was traced, and the SVG keeps that traced width and height.

### Is the SVG safe to put on a website?

The page writes the SVG itself from the traced shapes, then checks the text before offering it: only an svg element with filled paths, colours and coordinates may pass. A file with a script, a style or an outside reference would be refused.
