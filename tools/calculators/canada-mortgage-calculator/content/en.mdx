Canada Mortgage Payment Calculator works out the payment on a fixed-rate Canadian mortgage, with the rate compounded half-yearly, CMHC's minimum down payment and the mortgage loan insurance premium. It is for buyers in Canada who want to see what a home price, down payment and rate mean for each payment, whether they pay monthly, twice a month, every two weeks or weekly. The CMHC figures are linked on the page with the date we last reviewed them.

## How to use

1. Type the Home price and your Down payment. The calculator checks the down payment against CMHC's minimum: 5% of the first $500,000 and 10% of the rest.
2. Type the yearly Interest rate of your fixed-rate mortgage, up to three decimals, and the Amortization in whole years.
3. Pick a Payment frequency: monthly, semi-monthly, bi-weekly or weekly.
4. If your down payment is under 20%, a CMHC premium is charged on the loan. Choose whether it is added to the mortgage or paid separately as a lump sum.
5. Read the payment, the premium, the totals and the year-by-year summary of what you paid and what you still owe.

## Examples

The page opens on a $500,000 home with $25,000 down, the minimum. The loan of $475,000 is 95% of the price, so the CMHC premium is 4.00%, or $19,000, and the mortgage becomes $494,000 when it is added. At 5% over 25 years, compounded half-yearly, the monthly payment is $2,873.13 and total interest is $367,938.25 over 300 payments. Switch to bi-weekly and each payment is $1,324.59, 650 of them in all.

## Limits

This handles fixed-rate mortgages only, with the rate treated as compounded half-yearly; it does not handle variable rates. The premium uses CMHC's table for a traditional down payment and leaves out non-traditional down payments, portability, energy-efficient refunds and the provincial sales tax charged on premiums in Quebec, Ontario and Saskatchewan. It does not check the longest amortization a lender or insurer allows, accelerated payments, prepayments, renewals or other insurers' premiums. CMHC lists its loan-to-value bands to two decimals, such as 75.01% to 80%, and does not say which band a ratio between two of them, such as 75.005%, falls in; this calculator puts any ratio above 75% in the next band, and does the same at 65%, 80%, 85% and 90%. Payments and interest are rounded to the cent, so your lender's figures can differ by a few cents. Prices are up to $50 million, rates up to 30% and amortizations up to 40 years, limits we chose.

## FAQ

### Why does this give a lower payment than a US calculator at the same rate?

The Interest Act requires a mortgage to state its rate calculated yearly or half-yearly, and this tool treats the rate as compounded half-yearly. At 6%, that is about 0.494% a month instead of 0.5%, so $100,000 over 25 years costs $639.81 a month rather than $644.30.

### When is mortgage loan insurance needed?

CMHC says you need it when your down payment is less than 20% of the price. It is not available on a home of $1,500,000 or more, so the calculator asks for 20% down at that price.

### Is the premium paid upfront or over time?

Either. You can pay it as a lump sum or add it to your mortgage, and when it is added you pay interest on it with the rest of the loan.
